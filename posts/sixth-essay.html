I’m an Amateur at Everything and Ashamed About it

November 2026

And I do mean everything

I have been thinking about this for quite some time now. Myself, and many others I’m sure, have stopped doing things. A whole lotta people refuse to be amateurs at anything.


And I understand it perfectly, it’s embarrassing to be bad. We have been taught to associate any activity, even creative ones with productivity, with competence. The phrase of “if you’re not good at it/ not going to make a career out of it, why do it at all?” is very common. Most people are going to hear this from their parents or guardians, teachers or friends at some point. A knitter is expected to make beautiful sweaters and maybe start selling their knits. A writer is supposed to either carry you in a fantasy land or give you the best journalism you’ve ever seen, the only 2 “acceptable” kind of writers you can be. Why learn to play an instrument if you’re not going to, I don’t know, become a professional musician and do something with it.


Amateurs are rare creatures to find, people who continue struggling against mediocrity. They’re certainly better than me.


Am I an amateur writer? I’m certainly not a professional one, but with my infrequency to publish anything I’m not sure I’m even an amateur. I am by its definition, but I certainly feel worse than one sometimes.

They’re watching you through the walls

I swear I’m not schizophrenic, even though I do sometimes see shadows in the corners, but that’s just paranoia, my psychiatrist said so- which is a joke, mostly, but my point is:


Anyways, do you feel constantly watched? I feel like a lot of gen-z and gen alpha kids raised by the wild internet (and especially those of us “blessed” by unrestricted access) feel like we’re in the Truman show. I haven’t watched it, but I think I’m right in making this association. We’re constantly feeling like we’re being perceived, like there’s an audience that watches and judges our every move. It doesn’t matter that you’re dancing in your own room, with no one home, and all the curtains drawn, a lot of people will still feel ridiculous. We internalize people’s gazes, unconsciously, so much.


I sometimes wonder about what I do, even when I’m alone. Are the things I’m doing when I’m by myself things I want to do, or do I do things that I think will be perceived well by my imaginary audience? This might be either a very personal experience or something that someone will read and say “that’s literally me!”. I’m not sure which one is worse.

✦

I hate naturally talented people

I have realized that I’m a pretty hateful person. I don’t necessarily want to be, but I sometimes can’t help it. And I hate a lot of people that are “naturally talented”. Those kids that can play piano after 5 lessons and sound like Mozart’s reincarnation. I’m aware I’m exaggerating a lot, but I swear there’s this… mediocrity I feel that’s making me a horrible and hateful person. I hate that people can feel the rhythm of a song and naturally start moving their bodies like God created them for it. I hate the people that can understand hard sciences like math and chemistry easily, because it’s “logical”. Those people that came out of their mother’s womb and started doing backflips out of the doctors’ arms, because they’re just “naturally athletic”. I hate the people who have won the genetic lottery-and I’m aware this one isn’t a skill at all, but people who are conveniently attractive, because there’s nothing worse, especially as a woman, than being fucking ugly.


I wish I was “naturally good” at something. Maybe navigating life would’ve been easier, maybe it wouldn’t have been but at least I would be good at something.


I’m also aware that these little miracles happen rarely, and a lot of people work really hard to hone their crafts, but I’m a bitter person, and the audience (at least my imaginary one) loves the myth of natural talent. Is it my laziness and lack of determination to actually do anything making me a bitter person? Probably, yes, but it’s easier for me to complain about other people than to do anything about it. : ) (it really is easier)

✦

Growing up makes you smaller

I don’t mean that literally, of course, but your existence does. Children are loud, and confident, and creative and expressive, fearless and utterly careless. Of course, growing up you are supposed to change. As much as the internet made it a meme, we do live in a society and we are supposed to learn how to be considerate of other people, to learn boundaries.


But I do wonder what happened to that girl, the little me who was known to be such a loudmouth. According to my parents, I spent my entire childhood just…not shutting up. Constantly asking what people are doing, and why, and how; that was my number one objective. As a kindergartner I spent my summer bothering the poor handy-mans working on our house on how they’re isolating and painting and rewiring the electric cables (they were very kind and would entertain me for a while until my mother found me and dragged me outside by my ear, while apologizing profusely for my disturbance). Why the fuck was I asking that? I’m not sure, it’s not like I really understood a lot, but I was just so…curious.


I was not ashamed to ask questions. Frankly, I’m still not, my curiosity and my loudmouth were (and are) quite literally my only 2 defining character traits. But I’ve certainly become more aware. I’m much more aware of how annoying I can be, how that can cause a disturbance. I’ve quit so many things, because I’m just not good at them. I’m ashamed to pick up so many things, because I will be bad at them. Especially those that will require others to perceive how bad I am. This sounds like a pity party, but it’s my blog and I will make myself the birthday girl.

“Omg you people cant do anything”

I’m kind of lazy, and even though my depression does contribute to my… doing nothing- I’m still lazy. I’m lazy, and afraid, and bitter, and that’s not a good combination.


I hope this essay will make me less afraid to post, and my willingness to write this will make my laziness grow just a bit smaller.


I do hope that, when my frontal lobe develops and I mature, I’ll be comfortable with looking ridiculous for a bit, for the sake of a greater good. Go be an amateur as well, for as salty as I sound, there is satisfaction in it. Did you know that the word amateur comes from the Latin “amator”, meaning lover? Go love something, it’s good for you.
